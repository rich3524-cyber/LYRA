Six failed publishes, three root causes

Delivery reliability

Twenty-five publish attempts produced nineteen live posts. The six failures are not random — they are the same three problems firing twice each, and all three are fixable before the next batch.

The third is infrastructure and is the one to check first: the LinkedIn connector failed on both 24 and 28 July while the same account published successfully on other days, which points at an intermittent connection rather than a permanent break.

Three things worth your attention

Flagged for decision

Contradictory claim, both live
The same creative went out with two different numbers on the same day

On 28 July, LinkedIn and TikTok published 2 hours a week. Typing replies your clients don't pay for. Ten minutes earlier, Instagram, X and Facebook published the identical creative reading 12 hours a week.

Both are still live. For a product whose entire pitch is reclaimed hours, publishing two different figures for the same claim in the same hour is a credibility problem, not a typo. Decide which number is defensible and delete or edit the other.

Queued with an error
A misspelt hashtag is sitting in two scheduled Instagram posts

Both the 6 August and 14 August Instagram posts carry #SmaCllBusiness. It reaches no one and reads as carelessness on a founding-member announcement. Fix before the 6 August slot, which is imminent.

Positioning risk
The "first and only" claim is contestable against a tracked competitor

LYRA's highest-reach post of the period asserts that no platform responds to an audience automatically and that LYRA is the first. One of the two competitors tracked in this workspace, Sintra, markets an AI assistant explicitly framed around checking Facebook comments.

The distinction LYRA can defend is per-client brand-voice fidelity at agency scale, not primacy. Absolute firsts invite a rebuttal in the comments; a sharper claim about voice preservation does not.

Where to point the next sixty days

Options, not a single recommendation

Option A · Fix before you scale
Repair the pipeline, hold cadence flat

Reauthorise the LinkedIn connector, set an asset-format check before scheduling, and confirm whether X and TikTok metrics are actually reporting. Keep output at current levels for a fortnight and re-measure with a clean pipeline.

Trade-off: two weeks of no growth in volume, but every number after that is trustworthy. Right choice if you intend to report these figures to anyone.
Option B · Concentrate
Drop to Instagram and LinkedIn, double the depth

Those two channels produced 93% of measurable reach and every meaningful interaction. Redirect the effort currently going into X, TikTok and Facebook into more Instagram frequency and longer-form LinkedIn posts built around the "847 comments" framing that already worked.

Trade-off: you give up presence on channels that may simply be under-measured rather than under-performing. Reversible, but you lose the posting history if you come back later.
Option C · Keep coverage, change the assets
Stay on all five, stop cross-posting identical creative

Four of the six failures and the contradictory-claim incident all trace back to one creative being pushed to five destinations at once. Build native assets per platform — vertical video for TikTok, text-first for LinkedIn, static for Instagram — and the failure rate falls without cutting reach.

Trade-off: materially more production time per campaign for channels that have so far returned nothing measurable.

Regardless of which you pick

  • Resolve the 2-vs-12 hours claim before any further paid or organic push. It undermines the core proposition.
  • Fix the hashtag in the 6 August post — it goes out imminently.
  • Reframe the category claim from "first and only" to defensible ground on brand-voice fidelity.
  • Only three posts are queued past today. After a nine-day gap in publishing, the pipeline needs refilling now, not after the next planning session.
